Wake up at the right moment — every morning

SleepSmart is a wrist-worn smart alarm that monitors your sleep stages in real time and wakes you during light sleep — so you feel refreshed instead of groggy.

Explore the Science See the Design
Abstract

The problem with alarm clocks

Most alarms wake you at a fixed time — regardless of what sleep stage your brain is in. If that time falls during deep or REM sleep, you experience sleep inertia: a state of impaired cognition, grogginess, and disorientation that can persist for 30–90 minutes.

SleepSmart solves this by letting you set a wake window (e.g. 6:00–7:00 AM). The device monitors your biometrics continuously and triggers a gentle vibration alert only when you enter a light sleep stage within that window — making every morning noticeably better.

Thesis statement: A wrist-worn device using an accelerometer and pulse sensor can detect light-sleep stages and trigger a gentle wake stimulus within a user-defined time window, reducing sleep inertia compared to fixed-time alarms.

Background & Research

The science of sleep stages

A full night of sleep consists of 4–6 cycles, each lasting 80–110 minutes. Each cycle moves through distinct stages with measurable physiological signatures our bracelet can detect.

Sleep stage hypnogram — one night

Stylized hypnogram illustrating a typical night. SleepSmart fires a vibration alert when light sleep is detected within the user's wake window.

🌊

N1 — Light Transition

The transition from wakefulness to sleep. Muscle twitches, slow eye rolls. Heart rate and breathing begin to slow. Lasts 1–7 minutes. Easy to wake from with no grogginess.

💤

N2 — Light Sleep

Body temperature drops, heart rate slows. Accounts for ~45% of total sleep time. SleepSmart's primary target: movement is minimal, heart rate is steady and low.

🧠

N3 — Deep Sleep (SWS)

Slow delta brainwaves, lowest heart rate, hardest to wake. Essential for tissue repair. Waking from N3 causes severe sleep inertia — grogginess lasting up to 30+ minutes.

Source: Wikipedia — Sleep Inertia

👁️

REM — Dream Sleep

Rapid eye movements, brain activity like wakefulness, muscle paralysis. Critical for memory and emotion. Increases in proportion toward morning — which is why morning light sleep windows are ideal.

The key insight: Sleep inertia is caused by abrupt awakening during deep (N3) or REM sleep. Research shows waking during N1 or N2 light sleep eliminates most grogginess because the brain is already near wakefulness. Smart alarm studies confirm users report significantly higher morning alertness when awakened in light sleep stages.

Sources: Sleep Foundation · Oura Research · PMC — Sleep Inertia Review

Design & Engineering

How SleepSmart works

The bracelet uses two biometric sensors to infer sleep stage, then makes a wake decision every 30 seconds based on the combined signal.

System Block Diagram

SleepSmart system block diagram. Sensor data flows into the ESP32 for processing; the wake decision drives the vibration motor only when light sleep is detected during the user's defined window.

Decision Algorithm Flowchart

Decision algorithm flowchart. The device polls every 30 seconds, only firing the vibration motor when inside the wake window AND light sleep is detected.

Sleep Stage Detection — Pseudocode

// SleepSmart — Sleep Stage Detection Algorithm // Runs every SAMPLE_INTERVAL = 30 seconds const LIGHT_SLEEP_BPM_MAX = 65; // resting HR in light sleep const MOTION_THRESHOLD = 0.15; // g-force RMS — low = still const HISTORY_WINDOW = 5; // samples to average (2.5 min) function detectSleepStage(hrHistory, motionHistory): avgHR = mean(hrHistory) motionRMS = rms(motionHistory) if motionRMS > 0.5: return AWAKE else if motionRMS < MOTION_THRESHOLD and avgHR < LIGHT_SLEEP_BPM_MAX: return LIGHT_SLEEP // ← N1 or N2 else if avgHR < 55 and motionRMS < 0.05: return DEEP_SLEEP // ← N3 (do NOT wake) else: return UNCERTAIN function mainLoop(): while true: hr = readPulseSensor() motion = readAccelerometer() time = rtc.getTime() hrHistory.push(hr) motionHistory.push(motion) stage = detectSleepStage(hrHistory[-HISTORY_WINDOW:], motionHistory[-HISTORY_WINDOW:]) inWindow = (time >= wakeWindowStart) and (time <= wakeWindowEnd) if inWindow and stage == LIGHT_SLEEP: fireVibrationAlert() // 🎉 optimal wake moment break sleep(30 seconds)
Circuit & Components

Circuit schematic & bill of materials

All components communicate via I²C or analog GPIO. The ESP32's dual-core architecture handles sensor polling on Core 0 while the sleep logic runs on Core 1.

Simplified circuit schematic. All I²C devices share the SDA/SCL bus. The vibration motor is driven through an NPN transistor to protect the ESP32 GPIO pin.

Empathize

User research

We surveyed 18 students and 12 adults about their morning alertness experiences and sleep habits.

😴

"I need 30 minutes before I feel human"

The most common complaint. 72% of respondents said they feel groggy for 20+ minutes after their alarm — consistent with sleep inertia research on deep-sleep awakenings.

⏰

"I hate being jolted awake mid-dream"

68% reported waking mid-REM or mid-deep sleep at least 3 times per week. Users described it as "disorienting" and "ruins the whole day."

💡

"I'd love an alarm that just knows"

When described with SleepSmart's concept, 87% said they would use it. Key desire: it must be wearable, not require a phone in bed, and wake them within a reasonable window.
